# EX-03 findings: what the grid says about RQ1 to RQ3

Numbers are from `ex03_tables.md` (generated by `python -m wpf.grid_report`) and the figures `docs/figures/ex03_data_efficiency_{val,test}.png`
and `ex03_accuracy_vs_cost.png` (`scripts/analysis/plot_grid.py`). MASE is reported at H = 6 / 36 / 64 (1, 6 and about 10.7 hours
ahead); every recipe was chosen on validation and the test split was scored once per final model.

## RQ1: does PatchTST trained from scratch beat zero-shot Chronos?

On validation, yes at the long horizons and no at the short one. With all 134 turbines, PatchTST (quantile) reaches
0.273 / 0.551 / 0.628 against zero-shot Chronos-small at 0.267 / 0.593 / 0.698: 7% better at H = 36 and 10% at H = 64, 2% worse at
H = 6. The LSTM does the same (0.272 / 0.542 / 0.616). At the shortest horizon nothing beats the last value: persistence
scores 0.263, the same as Chronos.

On test the picture reverses. PatchTST at 100% scores 0.265 / 0.506 / 0.602 and the LSTM 0.259 / 0.501 / 0.609, while zero-shot
Chronos scores 0.239 / 0.473 / 0.590 and persistence 0.235 / 0.458 / 0.564. The models that won validation by 7 to 10% lose the
test by 2 to 7%, and persistence is the strongest forecaster of all on the test months. The two splits are different regimes:
every model's test MAE is lower than its validation MAE (Chronos 98 kW against 110 kW at H = 6), and the data say why
(`results/ex03_period_stats.json`, `scripts/analysis/period_stats.py`): mean power and wind speed are alike in the two periods (411
and 449 kW, 5.2 m/s), but power changes less over a horizon in the test months. The mean absolute change over 6 steps is 128 kW
on test against 154 kW on validation, over 36 steps 271 against 335 kW, over 64 steps 342 against 416 kW. The test period is
smoother, and in a smooth period the last value is hard to beat. The Chronos models, which had no validation-based selection,
also improve from validation to test (0.698 to 0.590 at H = 64), so the regime change affects every model; the from-scratch
models' validation edge is what did not carry over. The from-scratch models learned something that held on the validation months and
did not transfer. This is the main result of the project and the report has to carry both splits: the validation
ranking is what a practitioner would have chosen from, the test ranking is what they would have got.

## RQ2: how much of the gap do full fine-tuning and LoRA close?

Fine-tuning changes little. On validation the three Chronos variants sit within 0.01 MASE of each other at every fraction and
horizon, and LoRA r8 (0.59M trainable parameters) is indistinguishable from zero-shot (0.265 / 0.593 / 0.698 against 0.267 / 0.593 /
0.698). Full fine-tuning at 25% and 100% is at zero-shot level on validation and is the best Chronos variant on test (0.236 / 0.461 /
0.569 at 25%, a 2 to 4% gain over zero-shot). Full fine-tuning at 5% is the exception: it is worse than zero-shot at H = 36 and 64
(0.623 / 0.733 on validation, 0.531 / 0.674 on test), while LoRA at 5% is not. Seven turbines for 1000 full-parameter steps look
like long-horizon overfitting; CF-06 (#63) checks it with 200 and 500 steps.

So the gap between PatchTST and Chronos on validation is not closed by adapting Chronos: at the long horizons on validation
PatchTST stays 7 to 10% ahead of any Chronos variant. On test, where Chronos already leads, fine-tuning adds 2 to 4%.

## RQ3: data efficiency and cost

- **5% of the turbines (7 turbines).** Chronos wins outright. PatchTST from scratch is 0.395 / 0.725 / 0.794 on validation and the LSTM
  0.481 / 0.687 / 0.745 (with the largest seed spread of the grid, ± 0.03 at H = 6), against zero-shot 0.267 / 0.593 / 0.698. The
  pretrained model needs no training data to reach a level the from-scratch models need the full farm for.
- **25%.** PatchTST and LSTM catch Chronos at H = 36 on validation (0.620 / 0.604 against 0.593) and are still behind at H = 64.
- **100%.** PatchTST and LSTM pass Chronos at H = 36 and 64 on validation, and lose to it on test (RQ1).
- **Cost.** A PatchTST run is 0.24 to 0.91 SU (4 to 5 minutes of training on one V100) and an LSTM run 0.25 to 1.11 SU; a Chronos
  run is 4.8 SU, of which 1230 s is inference over the validation and test windows and about 200 s training. Zero-shot is 4.15 SU of
  pure inference. Per unit of accuracy on validation, the 3M-parameter PatchTST is the cheapest model at the long horizons and
  the 46M-parameter Chronos the cheapest at H = 6 (no training). LoRA trains 0.59M parameters instead of 46M for the same
  accuracy as zero-shot; its cost is the same as full fine-tuning because inference dominates.
- **Chronos-T5-base** (201M) is 0.263 / 0.590 / 0.696 zero-shot on validation, 0.004 better than small at the long horizons for
  three times the inference time; fine-tuned at 5% on 2 GPUs it shows the same 5% penalty as small (0.613 / 0.719). CF-06 adds the
  100% row.

## Caveats the report must state

1. **Validation and test disagree on the ranking** (RQ1). Both are reported; nothing was tuned on test.
2. **The Chronos seeds barely vary.** Std is at most 0.001 across the three seeds: 1000 steps at lr 1e-5 hardly move the weights
   and the forecasts are averaged over 20 samples. The three Chronos seeds show inference and training noise, not recipe
   robustness, and are not a seed study in the sense the PatchTST and LSTM seeds are (std up to 0.03 at 5%).
3. **Persistence matches Chronos at H = 6** on validation (0.263 for both) and beats every model on test at every horizon. At the
   shortest horizon the last value is the forecast to beat, and on the test months nothing beats it.
4. **The grid is larger than the interim report's 27 runs**: PatchTST point and the LSTM were added at every fraction and seed
   (46 cells). Every cell ran on one V100 so the SU figures are comparable; the grid cost 107.8 SU.
5. **Chronos-T5-base was run at 5% (CF-03) and 100% (CF-06) only**, one seed each, as the proposal's optional item.
6. **PatchTST point versus quantile.** The point head (MSE) is worse than the quantile head's median at every fraction on
   validation except 5% (0.369 / 0.654 / 0.727 against 0.395 / 0.725 / 0.794), where the pinball loss on seven turbines is the harder
   fit; with full data the quantile model is 1 to 2% better and also gives the WQL.
