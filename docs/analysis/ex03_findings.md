# EX-03 findings: what the grid says about RQ1 to RQ3

Numbers are from `ex03_tables.md` (generated by `python -m wpf.grid_report`) and the figures `docs/figures/ex03_data_efficiency_{val,test}.png`
and `ex03_accuracy_vs_cost.png` (`scripts/analysis/plot_grid.py`). MASE is reported at H = 6 / 36 / 64 (1, 6 and about 10.7 hours
ahead); every recipe was chosen on validation and the test split was scored once per final model.

## RQ1: does PatchTST trained from scratch beat zero-shot Chronos?

On validation, yes at the long horizons and no at the short one. With all 134 turbines, PatchTST (quantile) reaches
0.273 / 0.551 / 0.628 against zero-shot Chronos-small at 0.267 / 0.593 / 0.698: 7% better at H = 36 and 10% at H = 64, 2% worse at
H = 6. The LSTM does the same (0.272 / 0.542 / 0.616). At the shortest horizon nothing beats the last value: persistence
scores 0.263, the same as Chronos.

On test the picture reverses. PatchTST at 100% scores 0.265 / 0.506 / 0.602 and the LSTM 0.259 / 0.501 / 0.609, while zero-shot
Chronos scores 0.239 / 0.473 / 0.590 and persistence 0.235 / 0.458 / 0.564. The models that won validation by 7 to 10% lose the
test by 2 to 7%, and persistence is the strongest forecaster of all on the test months. The two splits are different regimes:
every model's test MAE is lower than its validation MAE (Chronos 98 kW against 110 kW at H = 6), and the data say why
(`results/ex03_period_stats.json`, `scripts/analysis/period_stats.py`): mean power and wind speed are alike in the two periods (411
and 449 kW, 5.2 m/s), but power changes less over a horizon in the test months. The mean absolute change over 6 steps is 128 kW
on test against 154 kW on validation, over 36 steps 271 against 335 kW, over 64 steps 342 against 416 kW. The test period is
smoother, and in a smooth period the last value is hard to beat. The Chronos models, which had no validation-based selection,
also improve from validation to test (0.698 to 0.590 at H = 64), so the regime change affects every model; the from-scratch
models' validation edge is what did not carry over. The from-scratch models learned something that held on the validation months and
did not transfer. This is the main result of the project and the report has to carry both splits: the validation
ranking is what a practitioner would have chosen from, the test ranking is what they would have got.

## RQ2: how much of the gap do full fine-tuning and LoRA close?

Fine-tuning changes little. On validation the three Chronos variants sit within 0.01 MASE of each other at every fraction and
horizon, and LoRA r8 (0.59M trainable parameters) is indistinguishable from zero-shot (0.265 / 0.593 / 0.698 against 0.267 / 0.593 /
0.698). Full fine-tuning at 25% and 100% is at zero-shot level on validation and is the best Chronos variant on test (0.236 / 0.461 /
0.569 at 25%, a 2 to 4% gain over zero-shot). Full fine-tuning at 5% is the exception: it is worse than zero-shot at H = 36 and 64
(0.623 / 0.733 on validation, 0.531 / 0.674 on test), while LoRA at 5% is not. The CF-06 step sweep (#63) shows the damage growing with the number of steps: the same 5% recipe
stopped at 200 steps scores 0.264 / 0.596 / 0.702 on validation and 0.239 / 0.487 / 0.611 on test, at 500 steps 0.263 / 0.604 / 0.710
and 0.240 / 0.503 / 0.635, at 1000 steps 0.623 / 0.733 and 0.531 / 0.674 at H = 36 / 64. Fewer steps bring the model back towards
zero-shot (0.593 / 0.698 and 0.473 / 0.590) and never past it: full fine-tuning on seven turbines has nothing to add, and every
step costs long-horizon accuracy. The same 1000 steps do no harm at 25% and 100%, so the damage comes from the sample, not the
schedule.

The question asked about the gap in either direction, and the answer has both. In the direction where PatchTST leads (validation,
H = 36 and 64), adapting Chronos closes none of it: PatchTST stays 7 to 10% ahead of every Chronos variant. In the direction where
Chronos leads (test, every horizon), fine-tuning widens the lead by 2 to 4%, LoRA by nothing. Both sentences belong in the report.

## RQ3: data efficiency and cost

- **5% of the turbines (7 turbines).** Chronos wins outright. PatchTST from scratch is 0.395 / 0.725 / 0.794 on validation and the LSTM
  0.481 / 0.687 / 0.745 (with the largest seed spread of the grid, ± 0.03 at H = 6), against zero-shot 0.267 / 0.593 / 0.698. The
  pretrained model needs no training data to reach a level the from-scratch models need the full farm for.
- **25%.** PatchTST and LSTM catch Chronos at H = 36 on validation (0.620 / 0.604 against 0.593) and are still behind at H = 64.
- **100%.** PatchTST and LSTM pass Chronos at H = 36 and 64 on validation, and lose to it on test (RQ1).
- **Cost, in GPU hours as RQ3 asked.** Training alone: a PatchTST run at 100% is 0.85 SU (254 s on one V100), an LSTM run 1.0 SU, a
  Chronos-small full fine-tune 0.7 SU (208 s) and a LoRA fine-tune 0.65 SU; zero-shot trains nothing. Whole job, training plus
  scoring the validation and test windows: 0.24 to 1.11 SU for the from-scratch models and 4.8 SU for every Chronos variant, because
  Chronos spends 1230 s sampling 20 forecasts per window; zero-shot is 4.15 SU of pure inference. So fine-tuning Chronos costs
  about what training PatchTST costs; using Chronos at all costs five times more, and that is the number a deployment pays. Per unit of accuracy on validation, the 3M-parameter PatchTST is the cheapest model at the long horizons and
  the 46M-parameter Chronos the cheapest at H = 6 (no training). LoRA trains 0.59M parameters instead of 46M for the same
  accuracy as zero-shot; its cost is the same as full fine-tuning because inference dominates.
- **One model per horizon** (the paper's practice, PT-03): with the lr 1e-4 recipe, separate H = 6 and H = 36 models score 0.273 and
  0.569 on validation against 0.275 and 0.568 for the shared H = 64 head trained the same way (0.226 / 0.463 against 0.228 / 0.465
  on test). The shared head loses nothing at the short horizons, which is why the grid uses one model.
- **Chronos-T5-base** (201M) is 0.263 / 0.590 / 0.696 zero-shot on validation and 0.239 / 0.476 / 0.599 on test: 0.004 better
  than small at the long horizons on validation and no better on test, for three times the inference time; fine-tuned at 5% on 2 GPUs it shows the same 5% penalty as small (0.613 / 0.719). Fine-tuned at 100% on 2 GPUs (CF-06) it
  scores 0.261 / 0.587 / 0.697 on validation and 0.233 / 0.457 / 0.568 on test: the best Chronos run on both splits, 0.002 to 0.003
  ahead of its own zero-shot on validation, 0.006 / 0.019 / 0.031 ahead of it on test (3 to 5% at H = 36 and 64, the largest
  fine-tuning gain of the project) and level with persistence on test (0.235 / 0.458 / 0.564). It costs 31 SU per run (3.5 SU
  of training on 2 V100s, then 69 minutes of sampling) against 4.8 SU for the small model: 2 to 4% at H = 36 and 64 on test for six
  times the compute.

## How solid is the reversal? Weeks, confidence intervals and a second fold

`scripts/analysis/test_breakdown.py` writes `results/ex03_test_breakdown.json`: MASE per test week (7 weeks of 7 days, mean over
the seeds) and, for the 100% rows, zero-shot and persistence, a 95% interval from 1000 bootstrap resamples of the forecast
origins (first seed of each cell), with the paired interval of the difference to zero-shot Chronos. `scripts/analysis/plot_findings.py`
draws the three figures of this section from the JSON files: `docs/figures/ex03_weekly.png` (test MASE per week), `ex03_bootstrap.png`
(the paired differences with their intervals, validation and test) and `ex03_two_periods.png` (the two held-out periods side by side).

- **The validation lead of the from-scratch models is real at H = 64 and marginal at H = 36.** PatchTST against zero-shot on
  validation: −0.072 at H = 64 with interval [−0.124, −0.017]; the LSTM −0.081 [−0.121, −0.038]. At H = 36 the PatchTST interval
  includes zero ([−0.097, +0.010]) and the LSTM's does not ([−0.089, −0.010]). At H = 6 nothing separates from zero-shot.
- **On test the from-scratch models are behind at H = 6 and 36 and level at H = 64.** PatchTST against zero-shot: +0.023
  [+0.012, +0.035] at H = 6, +0.034 [+0.007, +0.063] at H = 36, +0.013 [−0.021, +0.046] at H = 64; the LSTM the same pattern.
  So the test reversal is significant at the short and middle horizons and a tie at the long one.
- **Persistence is not significantly better than Chronos on test** (−0.024 [−0.062, +0.013] at H = 64) and not worse on
  validation (+0.032 [−0.011, +0.075]). The honest statement is that persistence and zero-shot Chronos are tied on both splits
  at every horizon, and only full fine-tuning at 100% edges zero-shot on test (−0.019 [−0.038, −0.001] at H = 64).
- **The weeks are not uniform.** At H = 64 PatchTST beats zero-shot Chronos in weeks 4 and 6 and loses in weeks 1, 2, 3, 5
  and 7; at H = 36 it loses in six of the seven. Persistence wins weeks 1 and 2 at H = 36, the two most volatile weeks. The
  reversal is not two bad weeks; it is the typical week, with the from-scratch models ahead only in the two calmest ones.
- **Second fold** (`scripts/analysis/fold2.py`, `results/ds02_splits_fold2.json`, `configs/*_fold2.json`): the split moved back
  by 25 days, so days 171 to 196, the grid's validation months, become a test period. PatchTST and the LSTM are retrained on the
  first 146 days with early stopping on days 146 to 171; Chronos needs no retraining, since nothing of its was chosen on those
  days, and its grid validation forecasts are rescored on the fold's MASE scale: zero-shot 0.264 / 0.587 / 0.690, full fine-tune
  100% 0.261 / 0.587 / 0.698, LoRA 0.263 / 0.588 / 0.694. PatchTST retrained on the 146 days scores 0.271 ± 0.005 / 0.547 ± 0.003 /
  0.620 ± 0.007 on the fold's test days and the LSTM 0.270 ± 0.004 / 0.536 ± 0.001 / 0.612 ± 0.004 (three seeds,
  `results/fold2_{patchtst,lstm}_100pct[_s2022|_s2023]_{val,test}.json`): 10 to 11% ahead of every Chronos variant at H = 64 and 7 to 9%
  at H = 36, with 25 fewer days of training data and nothing selected on those days (early stopping used days 146 to 171, where
  PatchTST scored 0.316 / 0.508 / 0.583 and the LSTM 0.309 / 0.491 / 0.566). The
  lead the from-scratch models had on days 171 to 196 was not selection; it is the period.
- **So the project has two held-out periods, and they disagree.** On days 171 to 196 the from-scratch models beat Chronos and
  persistence by about 10% at the long horizons, whether those days are the validation split (grid) or a test split nothing was
  chosen on (fold 2). On days 196 to 245 Chronos and persistence beat them by 2 to 7%, and nothing beats persistence. The honest
  conclusion is that on this farm the ranking of the model families depends on the regime of the period: persistence and zero-shot
  Chronos are a floor no model falls far below in either period, and the from-scratch models are the only ones that beat that
  floor by a wide margin in any period, and the only ones that fall below it in the other. The report presents both periods; a
  single test split, either one, would have told a wrong story.

## Caveats the report must state

1. **Validation and test disagree on the ranking** (RQ1). Both are reported; nothing was tuned on test.
2. **The Chronos seeds barely vary.** Std is at most 0.001 across the three seeds: 1000 steps at lr 1e-5 hardly move the weights
   and the forecasts are averaged over 20 samples. The three Chronos seeds show inference and training noise, not recipe
   robustness, and are not a seed study in the sense the PatchTST and LSTM seeds are (std up to 0.03 at 5%).
3. **Persistence matches Chronos at H = 6** on validation (0.263 for both) and beats every model on test at every horizon. At the
   shortest horizon the last value is the forecast to beat, and on the test months nothing beats it.
4. **The grid is larger than the interim report's 27 runs**: PatchTST point and the LSTM were added at every fraction and seed
   (46 cells). Every cell ran on one V100 so the SU figures are comparable; the grid cost 107.8 SU.
5. **Chronos-T5-base was run at 5% (CF-03) and 100% (CF-06) only**, one seed each, as the proposal's optional item.
6. **The second held-out period is 25 days** (fold 2, days 171 to 196) against 49 for the grid's test (days 196 to 245); the
   from-scratch models ran three seeds on it, the Chronos variants are the grid's single-seed forecasts rescored. The two periods disagree on the ranking (above), so the report presents two
   periods, not one test.
7. **The CF-06 rows are single runs.** The 200- and 500-step rows and the base 100% row are one seed each; the sweep checkpoints
   were scored twice, first with the filled context by mistake (`--context keep`) and then with the grid's `--context nan`, and
   the tables carry the second scoring. Their SU per run is the rescoring job plus the training time shown.
8. **PatchTST point versus quantile.** The point head (MSE) is worse than the quantile head's median at every fraction on
   validation except 5% (0.369 / 0.654 / 0.727 against 0.395 / 0.725 / 0.794), where the pinball loss on seven turbines is the harder
   fit; with full data the quantile model is 1 to 2% better and also gives the WQL.
